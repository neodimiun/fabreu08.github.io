Chemistry

Flasks, titrations, crystals, defensible data.

Before aerospace, I spent years in high-volume wet chemistry — environmental enforcement and pharmaceutical laboratories — where titration, ion chromatography, spectroscopy, and chain-of-custody were the job.

From municipal wastewater analysis to pharmaceutical-grade water systems, the method never changed: if the chemistry is not documented, it did not happen. That discipline is what I now bring to process baths and special-process control.

03 · M.S. Microbiology · Cleanrooms

Microbiology

Cells, colonies, ISO 4, validation studies.

I am a Master’s-level microbiologist (University of Florida). In sterile-drug manufacturing I executed disinfectant efficacy studies, environmental monitoring in ISO 4 cleanrooms, endotoxin testing, and microbial identification.

Microbiology taught me to treat invisible risk as engineering: sampling plans, contact times, isolates, and a report that will stand in an audit. The same posture now sits behind process control on the floor.

IT / Cyber

Infrastructure first. Traceability always.

The first professional chapter was information systems: hardware, networks, diagnostics, and keeping production systems alive. That is where I learned that a process is only as good as the record it leaves behind.

I still think like that — laboratory LIMS, process software, audit trails. Data integrity is not a slogan. It is the same systems instinct, applied to chemistry, microbiology, and now aerospace special processes.
